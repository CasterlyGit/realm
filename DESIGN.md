# REALM — Canon Bible v1.0

## 1. The Realm

This land is called **Hauthwen** — "the high burning" in the old tongue, though no one now living speaks it cleanly. It was a kingdom once, threaded together by the Drake-Oath: nine houses, nine dragon lines, one crown sitting at Carrick-na-Dun on the black peak. The Oath broke forty winters ago when the last Hauthen king was found cold in his solar with no wound on him and no heir in his bed. What followed was not war so much as a slow unstitching — vassals retreating to their valleys, dragons going feral or going home, roads forgetting themselves. Now three houses still keep their wyrms and their grudges, and the sky over the moor is the only court left where anything is decided. The crown is a rumor. The peasants pray to saints the bishops never approved. The dragons remember every slight across three generations and they settle them personally, in the air, above the bones of the country that used to feed them.

*You are not saving the world. The world ended already. You are finishing a sentence someone started before you were born.*

## 2. The Houses

**House Aelric** holds the eastern moors and what remains of the royal road. Their sigil is a black sun on smoke-grey — a single dark disc, no rays, set on field hex `#6E6963`. They were Master of Horse to the old crown, and when the king died they kept the stables and lost everything else. Aelric is *fallen but not yet poor* — they still feast, but on last year's salt-meat. They are known for breeding the black dragons, the Morren line, and for refusing to swear new oaths to anyone. The player is Aelric.

**House Calden** holds the white chalk cliffs to the south and the salt-trade that comes off them. Their sigil is a pale crescent, points-down, on deep blue — hex `#C9C2B4` on `#2B3A55`. Calden was a minor house under the king and is now *rising* fastest, having married into two of the broken houses and bought a third. They are known for the Skarn line — bone-pale dragons bred for cold endurance — and for never losing a duel they chose to fight. The enemy is Calden.

**House Brennoc** holds the western forest and the iron under it. Sigil: a forked antler in rust-red on bark-brown, hex `#8C3A2A` on `#4A3220`. Brennoc is *sworn* — formally to Calden by treaty three winters back, though half their banner-men deny it in private. They are known for the Vethrim line, the smoke-breathers, and for their long memory in matters of debt. Brennoc did not send a dragon to this duel. That absence is itself a message.

## 3. Dragon Roster

**Morren** — bonded to House Aelric. Size 1.0× (baseline). Scale color `#141014`, obsidian-glass, with a faint blue cast only at the wing-shoulder where the light catches. Wing membrane `#2A1A1A`, leathery and matte, ragged at the trailing edge with three healed tears from an older fight. Horns are swept-back and asymmetric — the left horn is longer and notched twice. Eye `#FF9A3C`, ember-orange. Breath: a tight cone of orange-white fire, `#FFB347`, with audible intake before each gout. Temperament: *patient, grudge-keeping, loyal beyond reason*. The Morren line is the old royal hunting strain, kept alive in Aelric stables by stubbornness alone.

**Skarn** — bonded to House Calden. Size 1.05×, slightly longer in the neck. Scale `#D8CFC0`, bleached-bone, with subtle banding visible only from above. Wing membrane `#E8DFCE`, veined with dark capillaries that show against the sky like cracked porcelain. Horns are paired and forward-curving, a goat's geometry. Eye `#6FB3C9`, pale ice. Breath: a sheet of white-blue plasma, `#B8D8E8`, cold-looking but it melts stone. Temperament: *calm, surgical, performative, cruel, certain*. The Skarn line was a gift to Calden from a fallen northern house and they have not let anyone forget the gift since.

**Vethrim** — bonded to House Brennoc. Size 0.85×, the smallest of the surviving lines. Scale `#4A3220`, bark-brown with darker striping. Wing membrane `#6B4A30`, leathery and thick, the trailing edge frilled rather than torn. Horns are short, blunt, and crowned in a ring of five. Eye `#C9A47A`, fog-gold. Breath: oily black smoke shot through with cinders, `#1E1A18` carrying `#FF6A2A` motes — blinds before it burns. Temperament: *cautious, ambush-minded, vindictive, slow to commit*. The Vethrim hunt in pairs in the old stories, though only one pair still flies.

**Iskari** — bonded to the fallen House Iskar, which no longer exists. Size 1.3×, the largest documented line. Scale `#2E4A6B`, deep storm-blue, almost black in shadow. Wing membrane `#5A7090`, tattered, with a translucent quality where light passes through old scarring. Horns are tall, straight, four to a side like a crown. Eye `#F0E68C`, lightning-yellow. Breath: a forking gout of pale lightning, `#E8F0FF`, that leaves the air smelling of struck flint. Temperament: *aloof, sky-bound, indifferent to riders, rare*. One Iskari is rumored to still nest in the eastern crags. No one has bonded one in sixty years.

**Tarne** — bonded to no house, the feral line. Size 0.7×, lean and fast. Scale `#7A5A3A`, dust-brown, with a green undertone at the belly. Wing membrane `#8C6A4A`, thin and almost dry-looking, like an old leaf. Horns are single, central, rhino-fashion. Eye `#2E5A3A`, marsh-green. Breath: a short, hot jet of acid mist, `#C9D88A`, that strips paint from shields at twenty paces. Temperament: *skittish, territorial, opportunistic, untrainable, vicious*. The Tarne were the peasants' dragons — small, cheap, and now wild on the moor in unsupervised broods.

## 4. The Rider

Her name is **Eira of Aelric**, twenty-three, second daughter and the one who could ride. Her older brother Cale was the heir and the better flier; he went up against a Skarn in the third week of last autumn and came down in pieces over the village of Carrn. The body was returned in a Calden cart with a note of condolence and a bill for the cart's hire. Eira has not slept a full night since. She is in this sky today not for the crown, not for Aelric, not for the realm — for the cart. For the bill. For the specific man who signed it.

## 5. The Adversary

His name is **Sten Calden**, twenty-eight, third son, and the man who signed the cart-bill. He and Cale were trained by the same dragon-master — old Halren of the eastern marches, who took both boys when their houses still spoke — and they were, for two summers, something close to friends. Sten chose his house. Cale chose his. Halren died last winter and neither of them attended. Sten does not hate Eira. He pities her, which is worse, and he intends to be quick about it because he believes that's the kind thing. He flies a Skarn called Veil. He has not lost yet.

## 6. Ground Denizens

**The Carrn-folk** — peasants fleeing the burning village in the western valley. From altitude they read as small ochre dots (`#B89060`, hooded cloaks) moving in broken lines along the cart-road, slow, clustered around something dragged. They are the human cost made visible.

**The Iron Riders** — Brennoc mounted knights patrolling the forest edge in column. From altitude: rust-red surcoats (`#8C3A2A`) on dark horses, moving in a disciplined line of eight. They do not look up. Their silence is the treaty.

**The Stonewardens** — grey-robed monks at the standing stones (`#A8A29A` robes, no faces visible from height) standing in a perfect ring inside the larger stone ring, unmoving. They are still keeping a vigil no one asked them to keep.

**The Sky-Carrion** — ravens of the dead, in loose drifting flocks (`#0A0A0A`, jagged silhouettes) circling whatever fell last. They follow the duel. They are the only audience.

## 7. The Landmarks

**Carrick-na-Dun**, the broken castle on the black peak. Royal seat of the Hauthen line. The great tower was shattered the night the king died — split clean down the eastern face — and no one agrees on whether it was lightning, a dragon, or something inside trying to get out. The throne is still in the hall, visible from the air as a pale square in the dark courtyard, because no one has yet been bold enough to claim or burn it.

**Carrn**, the burning village in the fog'd valley. A grain-and-wool village of seventy souls, Aelric-sworn, set fire eleven days ago by a Calden raiding party as a message. The visual telltale: the smoke is still grey, not black, and rises in three distinct columns from the granary, the chapel, and the headman's house — targeted, not random. The well is intact. Someone meant for the survivors to stay.

**The Naud Ring**, the standing stones on the moor. Twelve stones, irregular, set by people whose names are lost. Used for the Drake-Oath swearings in the old crown's time. They are not active in any magical sense — but flying low through them is bad luck in every story told about them, and every rider knows it.

## 8. Two More Landmarks

**The Drowned Bridge at Hareth.** A nine-arch stone bridge crossing what was a river and is now a dry stone bed — the river was diverted by Calden engineers six years ago to starve the eastern mills. The bridge stands intact over nothing, and from altitude reads as a pale comb laid across dark gravel. A line of dead willow stumps marks the old waterline. Birds nest under the arches because nothing hunts them there.

**The Bonefold.** A long shallow valley north of the moor where dragons have been going to die for as long as anyone has kept count. From the air it reads as a riverbed of white — bleached ribs and skulls in slow drifts, with the larger spines forming low ridges. No human goes in. The Tarne broods nest in the larger skulls. Flying over it, a rider's own dragon will go quiet.

## 9. Visual Style Bible

**Hero contrasts.** The four pairings that carry the picture: black dragon `#141014` against golden-hour sky `#FFD27A` — the duel's signature shot. Pale dragon `#D8CFC0` against fog'd valley `#C9A47A` — the enemy nearly vanishing into the weather, a deliberate threat. Castle stone near-black against sky zenith `#3A5A7C` — the broken silhouette on the peak. Ember-orange `#FFB347` against deep blue `#2B3A55` — fire at distance, the only true saturation in the frame.

**What looks gifted vs. amateur.** DO: silhouette-first design — every prop must read as one clean shape at 100m. DO: keep saturation low and warm across the board; let fire and eyes be the only chromatic events. DO: vary triangle density by importance — the dragons get geometry, the trees get six tris and a shadow. DO: bake long shadows into the ground texture; never rely on realtime shadow casting past the two principals. DO: trust the fog — depth is the cheapest atmosphere you have. DON'T: use pure black or pure white anywhere; everything sits between `#141014` and `#E8DFCE`. DON'T: high-saturation primaries — no red roofs, no blue water, break everything toward warm desaturated earth. DON'T: smooth shading on anything; flat-shade or it reads as a different game. DON'T: detail props that live below 50m of viewing distance. DON'T: ever animate a tree.

**Lighting rules.** One directional key light from the low sun, warm, casting long shadows. Ambient fill comes from the fog and is cool, the inverse of the key. The only emissive surfaces in the world are dragon eyes, breath weapons, the village fires, and the embers drifting from them — nothing else glows, ever. No more than three realtime point lights active at once and they belong to fire. The sun never moves during a duel.

**Typography and UI tone.** A weathered serif with sharp terminals — Cormorant Garamond or its kin — never a display font, never anything geometric. Title cards left-aligned, low in the frame, never centered. Diegetic text (a name, a date, a single line of dialogue) appears once, holds for three seconds, fades. The HUD is one mark: dragon health as a thin warm line at the bottom of the screen, no numbers. Silence is the default audio state. Music enters only at the death-blow and the end card. The end card is one line of serif text on black, held for five seconds, no logo.

## 10. The Opening Beat

The screen is black for a full second before the wind comes — a low, steady moor-wind with the dry rasp of distant fire underneath it. Then the fog parts and Eira is already in the air, banking east, the sun directly ahead of her at the angle where you cannot quite look at it. Below and to her left, Carrn is burning in three slow columns. Below and to her right, the Naud Ring sits dark on the moor. Ahead, very small against the gold, a pale shape is climbing toward her. A single line of serif text appears low in the frame and holds: *Eira of Aelric. For the cart.* It fades. Her dragon exhales once, audibly, and the duel begins.
